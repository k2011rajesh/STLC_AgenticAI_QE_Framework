# Requirements Quality Scoring

## Overview
Quality scoring for requirements ensures they are clear, complete, testable, and aligned with business needs.

## Scoring Criteria

### Clarity & Completeness (25 points)
- **Clear Language**: Requirements written in clear, unambiguous language (5 points)
- **Complete Information**: All necessary details provided (who, what, when, where, why) (5 points)
- **Acceptance Criteria**: Well-defined, measurable acceptance criteria (5 points)
- **Stakeholder Agreement**: Requirements validated and agreed upon by stakeholders (5 points)
- **Traceability**: Clear traceability to business objectives and user stories (5 points)

### Testability (20 points)
- **Verifiable**: Requirements can be verified through testing (5 points)
- **Measurable**: Quantifiable success criteria defined (5 points)
- **Unambiguous**: No room for multiple interpretations (5 points)
- **Feasible**: Technically achievable within constraints (5 points)

### Organization & Structure (20 points)
- **Prioritization**: Requirements properly prioritized (MoSCoW method) (5 points)
- **Dependencies**: Clear identification of dependencies and relationships (5 points)
- **Modular**: Requirements broken down into manageable, independent units (5 points)
- **Version Control**: Requirements version-controlled with change history (5 points)

### Business Alignment (20 points)
- **Business Value**: Clear business value and ROI justification (5 points)
- **User Needs**: Requirements based on real user needs and pain points (5 points)
- **Market Alignment**: Aligned with market needs and competitive landscape (5 points)
- **Regulatory Compliance**: Meets all relevant regulatory and compliance requirements (5 points)

### Maintainability (15 points)
- **Change Management**: Process for handling requirement changes (5 points)
- **Documentation**: Well-documented requirements with examples (5 points)
- **Review Process**: Regular review and validation cycles (5 points)

## Scoring Scale
- **90-100**: Excellent - Requirements are clear, complete, and testable
- **80-89**: Good - Minor clarifications needed
- **70-79**: Satisfactory - Moderate improvements required
- **60-69**: Needs Improvement - Significant gaps present
- **Below 60**: Poor - Major rework required

## Best Practices
- Use user story format: "As a [user], I want [feature] so that [benefit]"
- Include acceptance criteria for each requirement
- Regularly review and validate with stakeholders
- Maintain requirements traceability matrix
- Use collaborative tools for requirement management
